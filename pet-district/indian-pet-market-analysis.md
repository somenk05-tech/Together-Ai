### 1.4 The cat segment — the standout finding

| Metric | Value | Year | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cat food value | US$55.2M | 2020 | [AAFC, May 2026](https://agriculture.canada.ca/en/international-trade/reports-and-guides/sector-trend-analysis-pet-food-trends-india) |
| Cat food value | **US$213.9M (28% of pet food)** | 2025 | AAFC, May 2026 |
| **Cat food CAGR** | **31.1%** — fastest-growing segment | 2020–2025 | AAFC, May 2026 |
| Dog food value | US$540.1M (70%), 13.0% CAGR 2020–25 | 2025 | AAFC, May 2026 |
| Cat population share | 11% of pets (4.5M of 39.2M) | 2025 | AAFC, May 2026 |

**The structural insight:** cats are ~11% of the pet population but ~28% of pet food value, growing at 31.1% versus 13.0% for dogs. Cats monetise disproportionately because they are almost entirely fed packaged food, whereas dogs in India are largely fed household food.

This is confirmed commercially: Supertails reports that **cats contribute nearly 60% of its revenue despite being ~15% of India's pet population**, with the cat segment growing 30–40% annually versus 20–25% for petcare overall ([Indian Retailer, 2026](https://indianretailer.com/news/petcare-startup-supertails-targets-rs-200-cr-revenue-fy26)).

> **Caution:** Expert Market Research puts the India cat food market at [US$9.26M in 2025 growing at 8.20% CAGR](https://www.expertmarketresearch.com/reports/india-cat-food-market). This is ~23x smaller than the AAFC/Euromonitor figure and inconsistent with every other source. I would not cite it.

> **Note on scope conflict:** Mordor states dogs held 92.4% of the India pet food market in 2025, versus AAFC's 70%. The two are irreconcilable; AAFC's is Euromonitor-derived retail-sales data with a full segment breakdown, so I would anchor on it.

### 1.5 Premiumisation

- **Packaged-food penetration is the headline premiumisation stat:** only **3–5% of Indian pets' nutritional needs are met by packaged food**, versus ~70% in the US and 90%+ in Europe (Wazir, Sept 2025). The category's growth comes from converting home-fed pets, not from share-shifting between brands.
- **Product innovation (2020–2025):** 1,286 new pet food launches, rising from 151 in 2020 to **273 in 2025** (12.6% annual growth). The leading label claims were **"no additives/preservatives" (511 products)** and vitamin/mineral fortified (421). Dominant format: flexible stand-up pouches (537) (AAFC, May 2026).
- **Marketplace evidence:** Flipkart reported clean-label and breed-specific pet food growing **40%+ y-o-y**, with Gen Z and millennials making up 80%+ of pet-care demand ([MediaBrief, Apr 2026](https://mediabrief.com/flipkart-sees-50-growth-in-pet-care-category-fueled-by-rising-pet-parenting-trends/)).
- **Imports:** India's pet food imports were Can$192.9M in 2025, 16.2% CAGR 2020–25, with Thailand supplying 63.3% (AAFC, May 2026).

## 5. Regulatory / Trust Context

### 5.1 Pet food — a genuine regulatory vacuum

This is the single most important trust finding, and sources conflict, which is itself the story.

- **There are no mandatory standards for domestic pet food manufacture in India.** Per Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas: *"there are no mandatory standards for domestic production of pet food/nutrition"* ([India Corporate Law, July 2023](https://corporate.cyrilamarchandblogs.com/2023/07/barking-up-the-right-tree-a-closer-look-at-indias-pet-care-sector-and-regulatory-grey-areas/)). FSSAI does **not** directly regulate domestic pet food manufacture. Emerge Legal concurs: *"The pet food sector is not strictly regulated"* ([Emerge Legal](https://emergelegal.in/navigating-legal-regulations-in-indias-booming-pet-care-industry/)).
- **IS 11968:2019** — BIS specification, *"Pet Food for Dogs and Cats – Specification"*, covering nutritional adequacy, microbiological safety, chemical contaminant limits, labelling and traceability. **It is voluntary, not mandatory** (Cyril Amarchand, 2023; Emerge Legal). By contrast IS 2052:2009 (compounded cattle feed) *is* mandatory — showing regulatory intervention has followed human-health risk, not animal welfare.
- **Conflicting view:** [Food Research Lab, Oct 2025](https://www.foodresearchlab.com/insights/regulation-updates/fssai-pet-food-compliance-is-11968-2019/) states FSSAI *indirectly* enforces IS 11968:2019 via its general food-safety mandate (GMP, microbial testing, contaminant screening) and that manufacturers "must receive licenses to operate under FSSAI standards" — while conceding "pet food's legal categorization remains unclear within existing food and feed laws." **Treat FSSAI's scope over pet food as legally unsettled and say so in the strategy document.**
- **Pet Food Products of Animal Origin (Import into India) Order, 2008** applies **only to imports**, and only to dried processed food containing meat/avian ingredients for dogs and cats. It **excludes vegetarian and vegan pet foods** and all species other than dogs and cats (Cyril Amarchand, 2023).
- **Labelling** is policed under general law — the Legal Metrology Act for accuracy, plus consumer protection law against deception (Food Research Lab, Oct 2025; Cyril Amarchand, 2023).

### 5.2 Veterinary medicines — clearly regulated, with an online gap

- **The Drugs & Cosmetics Act, 1940 squarely covers veterinary drugs**: "drug" is defined to include *"all medicines for internal or external use of human beings **or animals**."* Veterinary clinics, hospitals and pharmacies selling, stocking or distributing animal medicines require licensure under the Drugs Rules, 1945 (Cyril Amarchand, 2023).
- **Marketers selling under their own brand share liability with manufacturers** per notification **GSR 101(E), 11 February 2020**. Manufacturers using third-party facilities must obtain **loan licences** (Cyril Amarchand, 2023).
- **Schedules H and H1** (notably antibiotics) require registration, record-keeping and restricted distribution; **Schedule X** substances additionally trigger **NDPS Act, 1985** compliance ([Pashudhan Praharee](https://www.pashudhanpraharee.com/prescribe-with-care-store-with-duty-legal-essentials-for-veterinary-drug-dispensing-in-india/)).
- **Schedule K exemption:** Registered Veterinary Practitioners may dispense without a separate sale licence **only** for animals under their direct treatment, must not run an open counter or commercial retail, must buy from licensed sources and maintain records (Pashudhan Praharee).
- **Online sale of veterinary medicines is legally unresolved.** The D&C Act contains no provisions for online drug sale; **draft amendments published in 2018 remain unfinalised**. The Delhi High Court prohibited online medicine sales; Madras High Court imposed then stayed a prohibition. Cyril Amarchand assesses these "would likely" extend to veterinary drugs (Cyril Amarchand, 2023).
- **Grooming products are a defined grey area:** the D&C Act's definition of "cosmetics" **excludes animals**, so dog shampoos sit in uncertain territory — but a grooming product making a **therapeutic claim (e.g. flea prevention) is regulated as a drug**, requiring drug licensure (Cyril Amarchand, 2023). **This is the sharpest claims boundary in the sector.**

### 5.3 Veterinary practice and telemedicine

- The **Veterinary Council of India Standards of Professional Conduct, 1992** ([full text, FAO Lex](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/IND221771.pdf)) governs practitioner conduct. It: restricts advertising and solicitation that projects superiority; **prohibits a vet from running an open shop for dispensing prescriptions written by other doctors**; bars prescribing "secret medicines" of unknown composition; **prohibits gifts, commissions or bonuses for client referrals**; requires fees be announced to the client at the time of service; and bars employing unqualified personnel to treat or operate.
- **The 1992 regulations predate digital communication and contain no telemedicine provisions whatsoever** (FAO Lex text). As of Jan 2023, there were **no official veterinary telemedicine guidelines** from the VCI or DAHD, and the human-medicine telemedicine framework has **not** been extended to veterinary practice ([Pashudhan Praharee, Jan 2023](https://www.pashudhanpraharee.com/veterinary-telemedicine-in-india-a-new-horizon-for-transforming-animal-healthcare/)). Every teleconsultation offering in §2 therefore operates without formal regulatory oversight.
- **Vet supply gap: 34,500 veterinarians available against 67,000 required** (Pashudhan Praharee, Jan 2023).
- The **referral-commission prohibition and the "no open shop for others' prescriptions" rule** are the two VCI provisions most likely to constrain a commerce-plus-vet business model. Any design that pays vets for prescription volume, or that fills prescriptions written by affiliated vets through a retail arm, needs legal review.

### 5.4 Animal welfare and retail

Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act, 1960 governs welfare, operationalised through the **Pet Shop Rules 2018** (Registration Certificate required to operate a pet shop) and the Breeding and Experimentation Rules 1998 under CPCSEA (Emerge Legal).

### 5.5 What this means for claims

**Permitted / low-risk:** compositional and nutritional statements that are accurate (Legal Metrology Act); "no additives/preservatives" style claims — the most common claim type in India, on 511 of 1,286 launches 2020–25 (AAFC, May 2026); voluntary conformity to IS 11968:2019, which is a **positive differentiator precisely because it is not required**.

**Not permitted / high-risk:** unsubstantiated therapeutic claims on grooming or non-drug products without drug licensure (Cyril Amarchand, 2023); advertising that projects professional superiority for vets (VCI 1992); referral commissions to vets (VCI 1992); marketing under own brand without accepting shared manufacturer liability (GSR 101(E), 2020). **Objectionable advertisement rules** under the Drugs and Magic Remedies Act, 1954 also apply to the sector (Pashudhan Praharee).

## 6. Gaps Together City Can Own

Each gap is tied to specific evidence above. I have not been briefed on Together City's model, so these are framed as market openings with the reasoning made explicit for you to test against your plan.

**1. Cat-first, not cat-also.**
Cats are 11% of India's pet population but **28% of pet food value, growing 31.1% p.a. against 13.0% for dogs** (AAFC, 2025 data). Supertails already sees **60% of revenue from cats at ~15% of population**. Yet the incumbent brand architecture is overwhelmingly dog-coded: JustDogs is dog-exclusive by name with 70% of revenue in food; Dogsee is "Dogsee Chew"; HUFT's imagery and personalisation catalogue lead with dogs. Only Zigly has a dedicated cat sub-brand (Catmos, litter). A cat-native brand, merchandising taxonomy and content system is available and defensible because renaming is not an option for the two most exposed competitors.

**2. The verified-standard trust play.**
There are **no mandatory domestic pet food standards** in India and **IS 11968:2019 is voluntary** (Cyril Amarchand, 2023). Sources as authoritative as a law firm and a food-regulatory consultancy openly disagree about whether FSSAI even has jurisdiction. This is an unusual situation: a category where the credible move — publish batch-level conformity to IS 11968:2019, third-party test results, and full ingredient provenance — is available to anyone but claimed by no one. Trust is purchasable here at a cost no competitor has paid.

**3. Subscription and replenishment — verified absent at the leaders.**
**Supertails, HUFT and Dogsee all lack a subscription programme** (verified on their own sites). Only Wiggles ("Wiggles Box") and Vetic (membership) have one. In a category where food is 59% of market value (Wazir, FY25) and consumption is perfectly predictable from pet weight and age, the absence of auto-replenishment across the market leaders is a straightforward retention gap. Dawgie Bowl's claimed **94% renewal rate on weekly packs** (Creature Companions, 2022) suggests the mechanic works in India when offered.

**4. Loyalty — an empty column.**
HUFT, after 18 years and 115 stores, offers a "Birthday Club" and nothing else. Supertails has no loyalty programme. Zigly's is unverified. In a repeat-purchase consumables category, the entire market is competing on discounting rather than accrued value. Note the constraint: any loyalty design touching veterinary referral must respect the **VCI 1992 prohibition on commissions or bonuses for client referrals**.

**5. Boarding and training — near-universal blanks.**
HUFT explicitly offers neither; Zigly offers neither per its own site; only Wiggles claims both. These are the two highest-frequency, highest-anxiety service needs of an urban pet parent and are served almost entirely by an unorganised local market. Services are already **24% of the pet care market** (Wazir, FY25). A city-dense operator is structurally better placed to aggregate boarding and training supply than a national e-commerce player, because both are irreducibly local.

**6. City-dense fresh food, solving the cold chain.**
Every genuine fresh player is metro-locked — **Doggie Dabbas' frozen meals reach only Delhi NCR and Mumbai** after 15 years; FurrMeals is Delhi NCR. The "pan-India" fresh brands have all retreated to retort or freeze-dried ambient formats with 2-year shelf lives. Nobody has built true fresh at scale. A city-by-city model with local production and short-radius delivery inverts this constraint rather than fighting it — and Supertails has already proved the enabling infrastructure works with **45 dark stores delivering in 30 minutes**.

**7. Compete against home-cooked food, not against brands.**
Only **3–5% of Indian pets' nutritional needs are met by packaged food, versus ~70% in the US and 90%+ in Europe** (Wazir, Sept 2025). Mars alone holds **51.1% of packaged pet food** (AAFC, 2024) — fighting for share of a 3–5% base is the wrong game. The addressable prize is the 95% still fed household food, and the product that wins it looks more like structured, vet-validated home cooking than like premium kibble. No competitor has framed itself this way.

**8. A defensible pharmacy and prescription layer.**
Online veterinary medicine sale is legally unresolved: no D&C Act provisions, 2018 draft amendments still unfinalised, conflicting High Court rulings (Cyril Amarchand, 2023). Meanwhile **HUFT — the largest retailer, 115 stores — offers no pharmacy at all**. A compliance-first pharmacy (genuine prescription capture, Schedule H/H1 controls, licensed retail entity, Schedule K boundaries respected) is slow and expensive to build, which is exactly what makes it defensible. The regulatory ambiguity is a barrier to entry for anyone unwilling to over-engineer for it.

**9. Structured pet profiles as the personalisation substrate.**
Verified personalisation in the market is either **cosmetic** (HUFT embroiders the pet's name on a bed) or **manual** (Wiggles' dietary counselling, Nuts Over Mutts' customised meals). Nobody has built a persistent breed/age/weight/condition profile that drives ration sizing, replenishment cadence, and clinical history together. Flipkart's data shows breed-specific food growing 40%+ y-o-y (Apr 2026), so the demand signal is real. This is also the asset that makes gaps 3, 6 and 7 compound rather than sit as separate features.

**10. Tier-2 cities, ahead of the incumbents.**
Flipkart reports that **Bangalore, Delhi and Kolkata together are only 10% of pet care orders**, with emerging demand in Mysore, Dehradun and Cuttack (Apr 2026). Supertails' dark stores cover **three cities**; its clinics are Bengaluru-only. HUFT is in 18+ cities, Zigly 19. Every full-stack player is metro-concentrated while demand is visibly dispersing. A city-replicable playbook — rather than a national platform — matches the actual shape of the market.

**11. The veterinary supply constraint is a moat, not an obstacle.**
India has **34,500 veterinarians against 67,000 required** (Pashudhan Praharee, Jan 2023). Vetic is spending US$66M to reach 500 vets; Supertails is spending Rs 60L–1 Cr per clinic. Vet capacity is the binding constraint on every full-stack model, and it is bought with capital and time — meaning any position built on privileged access to vet supply (employment terms, training pipelines, city-level exclusivity, or asynchronous models that raise vet throughput) is durable. Note that teleconsultation currently operates **without any formal VCI or DAHD guidelines**, so building to a self-imposed clinical standard now is both a trust asset and insurance against the rules that will eventually arrive.
